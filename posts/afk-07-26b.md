## Pre-War Disaster Fiction

I was listening to an episode of Ologies about societal collapse, and the expert attributed his interest in the subject to his Cold War childhood. This got me wondering whether modern, pre-war society was similarly interested in the apocalypse. ChatGPT suggested a few titles, which I've then researched manually:

<li><i>The Last Man</i>, 1826 work by Mary Shelley, brings back the bubonic plague. The pandemic and floods lead to clashes of refugee groups trying to settle the remaining land. Wikipedia calls it one of the first pieces of dystopian fiction. <br/>
Modern analogue: Greenland 2: Migration
</li>
<li><i>After London</i>, 1885, takes place in the aftermath of some space phenomena which shifted Earth's axis. London returns to nature and feudalism. <br/>
Modern analogue: Threads
</li>
<li><i>The Purple Cloud</i>, 1901, where a polar explorer returns to find everyone has died. There is a supernatural element to why he is chosen to survive. He spends years building a palace for himself before sailing to Constantinople and finding a "Circassian" woman who was born in a sealed shelter and never learned to speak. She is apparently destined to be his Eve? <br/>
    Modern analogue: The Pink Cloud
</li>
<br/>

## Revolving Circles of Malaysian Monarchy

I've previously mentioned Malaysia's rotating monarchy, where the current head of state is the Sultan of Johor (the state bordering Singapore). But on Reddit I learned that the king from Negeri Sembilan is also unconventional - he is elected from local families by a council of four ruling chiefs (Undang Yang Empat).

In April 2026 there was a court ruling to replace their monarch, Muhriz. His supporters countered that one of the council members on that court who signed the ruling had already been removed. Muhriz has continued to occupy the official residence and role, recently welcoming members to replace the council. The faction in favor of deposing him announced a new king in June, but the coronation had to take place at a hotel. The national Conference of Rulers was postponed, presumably to avoid taking sides. <br/>
Assuming a normal rotation, the king from Negeri Sembilan will become head of state in 2034.

There isn't much material online to explain this crisis. A pseudonymous Substack publishes updates and research on, for example, why the  council chief was removed: https://www.anaknogori.com/p/what-are-the-33-offences-mubarak They also have an Instagram illustrating things with Lego people (I assume that's inspired by Iran's social media strategy). <br/>
The writer's point-of-view is admittedly in one direction: that the council has "never, not once, publicly stated what [Muhriz] actually did wrong" and they claim that social media shows overwhelming public support.
